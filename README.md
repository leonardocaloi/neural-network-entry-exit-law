# An entry–exit law for the resurrection of dead neurons under smoothed ReLU activation

Research repository for an independent mathematical and computational study of
neural-network training dynamics: what happens to a "dead" unit (negative
pre-activation on every datum) when the hard ReLU is replaced by a smooth
approximation such as softplus, and gradient flow is followed into the
sharp-activation limit.

**Status.** Independent research by a single author. The manuscript has not
been peer-reviewed. Large language models assisted with derivations, code,
adversarial review and editing (see [AI assistance](#ai-assistance)). The
public-release review and its limits are recorded in
[`docs/REVIEW.md`](docs/REVIEW.md).

- Manuscript (PDF, 66 pages): [`06-notas/paper/paper-v4.pdf`](06-notas/paper/paper-v4.pdf)

## Overview

A ReLU unit whose pre-activation margin is negative on every training point
contributes nothing to the network output and, with the usual convention
ReLU′(z) = 0 for z ≤ 0 and fixed inputs, receives no gradient through its own
parameters. In discrete training such units stay dead; this is one of the
mechanisms discussed in the dormant-neuron and loss-of-plasticity literature.

Real networks are often trained with smooth activations (softplus, GELU). The
question addressed here is what replaces the absorbing state when ReLU is
regularized by softplus with stiffness β and the stiffness is sent to
infinity along gradient-flow trajectories.

The answer proved for a minimal configuration is an **entry–exit law**: in
the limit, the dead unit freezes and is released at the first time the signed
time integral of the residual generated by the rest of the network, taken from
the moment of death, returns to zero. The dead state behaves as a gate with
memory rather than a trap. The release time at finite β carries an explicit
first-order correction whose coefficient depends only on the residual at exit.

## Main results, by level of support

The manuscript keeps three levels separate. The repository is organized the
same way.

**Proved (Theorems 1 and 2, Appendix A and B of the manuscript).**

- *Theorem 1 (entry–exit law).* For a two-unit softplus network under gradient
  flow, one dead unit A and one live unit B, a fixed readout, one-dimensional
  inputs and hypotheses (H1)–(H4) (a transversal death, a hybrid residual whose
  signed area returns to zero, the other data exponentially inactive, a single
  episode), the trajectory converges to a hybrid limit at rate O(log β / β)
  away from the two switching instants, and the first return of the margin to
  zero satisfies

  $$t^{\beta}_{\mathrm{release}} = t_{\mathrm{exit}} - \frac{1}{c\,R^{\mathrm{hyb}}(t_{\mathrm{exit}})}\cdot\frac{1}{\beta} + o(\beta^{-1}).$$

- *Theorem 2 (forcing form).* The same law, rate and coefficient hold when the
  live side is any affine subsystem of order m (any number of live units with a
  constant activation pattern and a fixed readout), with constants uniform up to
  a factor O(√m) and an explicit threshold β₀ = O(m).
- *Proposition (branch selection).* The smooth-flow limit differs, after the
  exit time, from the absorbing hard-ReLU continuation obtained with the
  ReLU′(0) = 0 convention; the smoothed unit returns, the frozen one does not.
- *Corollary.* For an MLP of the affine class with fixed readout, the law at a
  resurrection episode follows from Theorem 2 once (H1′)–(H2′) are checked on
  the hybrid trajectory.

One step is declared open in the manuscript: the o(1) in the entry-corner
lemma is not quantified, so the remainder is o(β⁻¹) rather than the
O(log β / β²) the numerics suggest.

**Verified numerically (theorem-level numerics).**

- On a reference instance the measured release coefficient converges to the
  predicted −1/(c R(t_exit)) as β grows.
- A sweep of 600 instances drawn from a fixed prior retained 57 that satisfy
  (H1)–(H4) (340 have no death within the horizon, 162 have an area that never
  returns to zero, 41 die outside the detector window). On all 57 the release
  precedes the hybrid exit time; the relative error of the release coefficient
  against each instance's own prediction has median 2.68%, 90th percentile
  7.26% and maximum 12.01%, over predicted coefficients spanning a factor of 20
  (−91.8 to −4.5). The error is biased: 55 of 57 measured offsets are smaller in
  modulus than predicted (median ratio 0.975), read as the finite-β remainder.
- For the public release, three archived instances (m = 4, 8, 16) were
  re-integrated with an adaptive high-order integrator (SciPy DOP853) instead
  of the historical fixed-step Euler scheme; at β = 3·10⁵ the measured
  coefficients are within 0.4% of the predictions
  (`verification/check_entry_exit.py`).

**Empirical (pre-registered experiments on small trained networks).** The
experiments train the readout, use several active data at once and, in the
second confirmatory, a transformer block. For these the manuscript reports
observations, not consequences of the theorems. See the next section.

## Mathematical intuition

Write the shallow network as f(x) = Σₖ vₖ g_β(wₖx + bₖ) with
g_β(z) = β⁻¹ log(1 + e^{βz}), so g_β → ReLU as β → ∞ and
g_β′(z) = σ(βz) is the logistic function. Let z be the margin of the dead unit
on the datum that killed it, and let R^{hyb}(t) be the residual produced by the
live subsystem alone while the dead unit is held frozen. Define the signed area

$$\mathcal{A}(t) = \int_{t_{\mathrm{hit}}}^{t} R^{\mathrm{hyb}}(s)\,ds .$$

In the fast variable u = e^{−βz} ≫ 1 (the unit is dead when z < 0), the
quantity Ψ(u) = log u + u satisfies an exact additive identity along the flow,

$$\frac{d\Psi(u)}{dt} = -\,c\,\beta\,R(t) + c\log(1+e^{\beta z}) + \mathcal{E}(t),$$

where c is a constant of the active datum and the error term collects the
exponentially small contributions of the other data. Integrating from the
death time, Ψ(u(t)) − Ψ(u(t_hit)) ≈ −cβ 𝒜(t) up to O(log β) corrections. While
the area is negative the margin is pushed to a sticking depth
z ∼ −β⁻¹ log β and the unit's own gradient is exponentially small, so it is
effectively frozen; when the area returns to zero the margin is released and
crosses back to z > 0. The two corners (entry and exit) each contribute the
reciprocal of their own transversal speed to the first-order correction; the
entry contribution cancels against the area it cuts, leaving
−1/(c R^{hyb}(t_exit)). The law fails without the sign change of R^{hyb}, which
is why (H1)–(H2) are essential while (H3)–(H4) only simplify the proof.

Under hard ReLU with the ReLU′(0) = 0 convention, the unit's own gradient is
identically zero once all its margins are non-positive, so the frozen
continuation stays frozen on the whole window: the two constructions differ
after t_exit. GELU is outside the class covered by the proofs because its
derivative changes sign at z* = −0.7518.

## Experimental evidence

All runs are deterministic, single-threaded CPU runs at T = 0 (plain gradient
descent, no noise) with seeds, configurations and kill conditions frozen in
version-controlled JSON before execution. The archived per-run records of both
confirmatories are included and checksummed (see `docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md`).

**The ladder before the confirmatories (negatives are kept).**

- A reduced deterministic-schedule model was falsified under SGLD
  (`05-experimentos/p5-relu-morte-v2/`).
- Two instrument designs failed their own pre-registered null control: the
  600-run `etapa-4a` instrument was declared invalid because the pure-diffusion
  arm passed the calibrated test; the `4a.2` redesign was rejected at the
  design stage (`05-experimentos/NEGATIVOS.md`, `06-notas/2026-07-24-etapa4a-invalido-instrumento.md`).
- A pressure pilot (64 runs) first showed the mechanism in a trained MLP; two
  rate pilots (240 and 160 runs) sized the confirmatories.

**MLP confirmatory 4a.3** (`05-experimentos/etapa4-mlp-populacao/pilotos-4a2/pressao/taxa/`).
400 runs: four activation arms (softplus β = 3, 10, 300; hard ReLU) × hidden
width m ∈ {4, 6} × 50 seeds, horizon 5·10⁵ steps. Pre-registered estimands
and kill conditions in `config_4a3.json`.

| Estimand | Pre-registered kill condition | Result |
|---|---|---|
| C1, return probability given an eligible dynamical death, softplus β = 3 | CP upper bound < 0.076 | 15 returns / 30 eligible deaths, p̂ = 0.500, 95% CP [0.313, 0.687] (16 / 35 over all dynamical deaths, 0.457 [0.288, 0.634]) |
| C2, area opens before the return, softplus β = 3 | CP lower bound < 0.5 | 13 / 16 returns |
| C4, return probability against β | estimation only | monotone: 0.457 (β = 3), 0.370 (β = 10), 0.211 (β = 300) |
| V, validity (hard-ReLU arm) | any ground-truth return invalidates the run | 17 dynamical deaths, 0 returns |

The temporal ordering in C2 is partly structural (the clock accumulates area
only on dead steps); the empirical content is that the area opens at all,
which it does in 13 of 16 returns.

**Transformer confirmatory e5** (`05-experimentos/etapa5-transformer/`).
800 runs on a one-layer transformer block (d = 16, 2 heads, 4 feed-forward
units, 24 sequences of 3 tokens): four activation arms (softplus β = 3, 10;
GELU; hard ReLU) × attention trainable or frozen × injection depth
z ∈ {−0.3, −2.0} of a deliberately killed feed-forward unit × 50 seeds,
horizon 2·10⁵ steps. Five estimands were frozen in `config_e5.json`.

| Estimand | Result |
|---|---|
| E-A, validity: no sustained return with hard ReLU and frozen attention | 0 / 100 |
| E-B, upstream channel: p(sustained return, attention trainable) − p(frozen) | positive in all four arms: 0.210, 0.200, 0.220, 0.200 (bootstrap lower bounds ≥ 0.11); hard ReLU 20 / 100 against 0 / 100, Fisher exact p = 6.6·10⁻⁷ |
| E-C, own channel (softplus β = 3, frozen attention, shallow injection) | 22 / 50 sustained returns, p̂ = 0.440 [0.300, 0.587]; area opened before the return in 21 / 22 |
| E-D, GELU at shallow vs deep injection | 29 / 50 vs 0 / 50, but softplus β = 3 also gives 0 / 50 at the deep injection: confounded with depth, cause not established |
| E-E, β ordering of the MLP staircase inside the block | reproduced |

Two observations from the transformer stand on their own: hard-ReLU units that
cannot move through their own channel do return when the attention upstream
of them is allowed to move (E-B), and GELU′ is negative below z* = −0.7518
while GELU(z) is strictly negative for z < 0, so GELU has no zero-output dead
state of the ReLU type. The manuscript does not claim a proven GELU barrier;
the separating depth sweep is specified in its Appendix and left as future work.

An exploratory 13-instance pilot under Adam (`05-experimentos/e7-adam-portao/`)
is reported as exploratory only.

## Reproduction

Quick start (CPU only, about a minute):

```sh
python -m venv .venv && . .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
python verification/smoke.py             # SHA256 manifests, frozen analyses, sweep summary
python verification/check_entry_exit.py  # SymPy identity + DOP853 entry-exit checks
```

With PyTorch installed (`pip install torch==2.13.0 --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cpu`):

```sh
python verification/smoke.py --engines   # engine parity, determinism, frozen-ReLU null
```

## Limitations

- The theorems cover a minimal configuration: one dead unit, one active datum
  at a time, a fixed readout, one-dimensional inputs; Theorem 2 relaxes only the
  size of the live subsystem (constant activation pattern required). The
  trainable readout used in every experiment is outside the proved class.
- One proof step is open (the unquantified o(1) of the entry-corner lemma).
  The proofs were produced with LLM assistance and are checked only by the
  author's symbolic and numerical scripts; no independent verification exists.
- The regime is deterministic gradient flow (T = 0). Where probed, stochastic
  dynamics falsifies the deterministic schedule; the stochastic case is open.
- The trained networks are small (MLP with at most 6 hidden units; a one-layer
  transformer block with 4 feed-forward units). Nothing here supports claims
  about production-scale networks or about biological neurons.
- The GELU "barrier" is a derivative sign change, not a proven terminality
  result; in the transformer it is confounded with injection depth.
- The law is proved for softplus; persistence under erf smoothing is checked
  numerically only; GELU is outside the class.
- Commit hashes cited in the manuscript refer to the private research history;
  `docs/provenance/commit-map.tsv` maps them to this repository. Commit dates
  are the author's local history, not an independent timestamp.
